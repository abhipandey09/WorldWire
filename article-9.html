Deadly Houthi Missile Strike on Riyadh Airport Disrupts Flights, Killing Three

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 10, 2026 at 07:40 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Associated Press
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Associated Press editorial archive
Executive Briefing: A ballistic missile strike claimed by Yemen's Houthi forces hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, killing three people including a commercial pilot and damaging a passenger jet on the tarmac. The deadly breach has prompted major international carriers to suspend flights to the Saudi capital and triggered urgent regional aviation safety advisories.

A ballistic missile barrage launched by Yemen's Houthi movement struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, killing three Saudi nationals—including commercial airline pilot Captain Hamoud Ali Alkalthami—and wounding several travelers of multiple nationalities. According to statements from the kingdom's General Authority of Civil Aviation and the national carrier Saudia, the assault targeted airport facilities and caused direct structural damage to a commercial passenger aircraft stationed on the apron. Plumes of smoke were seen rising over terminal perimeters, and more than 120 domestic and international flights were abruptly canceled or diverted as emergency protocols took effect in the deadliest attack on Saudi civilian infrastructure in months.

The escalation marks a sharp retaliation following a renewed offensive launched last weekend by the Saudi-led coalition against rebel strongholds in Yemen. While the Saudia Group confirmed the loss of its pilot and emphasized that the damaged airliner held no passengers at the moment of impact, the Saudi-led coalition stated it destroyed three mobile missile launch platforms in response. Houthi military officials claimed full responsibility for the ballistic strike, declaring that logistical hubs and transportation infrastructure across the Arabian Peninsula remain active targets until coalition campaigns cease.

The direct strike on Saudi Arabia's primary aviation hub has triggered immediate global operational fallout. Major international carriers, including the Lufthansa Group, Air India, FlyDubai, and Pakistan International Airlines, announced immediate suspensions of service to Riyadh. Concurrently, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) issued updated conflict-zone guidance warning of severe hazards to civil aviation from deep-penetration strikes and heightened air-defense activations. With Riyadh scheduled to host a high-level summit of global energy executives in the coming days, the incident threatens commercial transit corridors and injects severe volatility into wider Middle Eastern security calculations.
