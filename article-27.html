Trump Calls to Oust Zelenskyy After Striking Landmark Russian Diesel Deal

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 11, 2026 at 04:26 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Reuters
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Reuters editorial archive
Executive Briefing: U.S. President Donald Trump has publicly called for Ukraine to replace President Volodymyr Zelenskyy following Kyiv's furious condemnation of a new Washington-Moscow energy agreement. The controversial deal waives sanctions to import millions of tons of Russian diesel, sparking a major rift between the United States, Ukraine, and European allies.

WASHINGTON/KYIV — Relations between Washington and Kyiv plunged into an unprecedented crisis this weekend after U.S. President Donald Trump urged Ukraine to remove President Volodymyr Zelenskyy from power, claiming the wartime leader has prolonged the conflict and disrupted global energy markets. Speaking to reporters on the South Lawn, Trump declared that it was 'time for Ukraine to get a new president' who is willing to strike a peace deal with Moscow, accusing Zelenskyy's military strikes on Russian fuel refineries of driving domestic U.S. diesel prices past historic thresholds ahead of the upcoming midterm elections. The remarks followed Friday's announcement by the White House and the U.S. Department of the Treasury granting emergency sanctions waivers to import an initial 300,000 tons of Russian diesel, with plans to scale deliveries to over 4 million tons in the coming months.

The surprise energy agreement triggered fury across Kyiv and European capitals, coming as U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were engaged in multilateral security consultations in Miami. President Zelenskyy forcefully condemned the waiver as a 'gift' to Vladimir Putin, warning that funneling oil revenues into the Kremlin's coffers effectively bankrolls Moscow's war effort and undermines Western leverage. Hours after the announcement, Russian forces launched a barrage of guided aerial bombs and missile strikes across Zaporizhzhia and Kyiv, killing at least 20 civilians in what Zelenskyy called Russia's bloody 'thank you' for sanctions relief. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha pushed back against Washington's rhetoric, asserting that Zelenskyy maintains the democratic mandate of the Ukrainian people and that leadership decisions reside solely in the hands of the electorate.

The deepening schism marks the most acute transatlantic fracture since Russia's 2022 invasion, threatening the consensus that has anchored Western military and financial support for Ukraine. In Washington, bipartisan pushback mounted swiftly on Capitol Hill, where lawmakers introduced legislative efforts invoking historic national security doctrine to overturn the executive sanctions waivers. European leaders are convening emergency diplomatic sessions in Brussels to assess the fallout, expressing acute concern that unilateral U.S. energy purchases could permanently fracture the G7 sanctions coalition and embolden Russian territorial maximalism on the eastern front.
