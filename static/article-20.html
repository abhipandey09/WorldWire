Saudi Coalition Strikes Yemen Targets After Fatal Riyadh Airport Missile Attack

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 10, 2026 at 10:59 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Reuters
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Reuters editorial archive
Executive Briefing: A Saudi-led coalition launched extensive retaliatory airstrikes across Yemen following a fatal Houthi ballistic missile attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport. The cross-border assault killed three people and damaged a commercial aircraft, prompting international airlines to suspend flights to the kingdom amid spiking regional tensions.

The Saudi-led military coalition launched a sweeping wave of airstrikes against Houthi positions across Yemen after ballistic missile strikes targeted King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, killing three people and leaving several wounded. The assault severely damaged a Saudia Airlines passenger jet parked on the tarmac and sparked thick smoke columns over the capital's primary transit hub, leading global carriers including Lufthansa Group and Air India to suspend commercial flights into Riyadh. Saudia confirmed that airline pilot Capt. Hamoud Ali Alkalthami was among those killed in what marks one of the deadliest strikes inside Saudi territory since regional fighting flared anew.

Coalition spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki declared that the attacks on civilian facilities "will not go unpunished," announcing targeted strikes against dozens of Houthi sites spanning Sanaa, Saada, and Hodeidah that eliminated ballistic missile launch platforms and underground munitions depots. In contrast, Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree issued an ultimatum warning international airlines to avoid Saudi airspace entirely, labeling it an active operational theater in retaliation for Saudi support of Yemeni government offensives near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The direct strikes against Riyadh's critical aviation infrastructure represent a dangerous escalation in the broader regional showdown, rattling global energy markets and raising alarm among international security officials. With Saudi Arabia scheduled to host high-level international energy conferences, the threat of sustained long-range missile and drone strikes poses acute disruption to commercial flight paths and maritime trade bottlenecks across the Red Sea corridor.
