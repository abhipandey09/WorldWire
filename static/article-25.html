South Korea Unveils $747B 'K-GX' Plan to Overhaul Heavy Industry and Scale Clean Power

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 10, 2026 at 11:03 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Reuters
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Reuters editorial archive
Executive Briefing: South Korea has launched an ambitious 10-year, 1,000 trillion won ($747 billion) green transformation strategy known as 'K-GX' to overhaul its manufacturing core and expand clean energy capacity. The initiative combines public fiscal outlays, vast climate financing, and private sector commitments to decarbonize heavy emitters and achieve 100 gigawatts of renewable energy by 2030. The move marks one of Asia's largest state-backed decarbonization drives aimed at decoupling industrial economic growth from fossil fuel reliance.

The South Korean government has officially unveiled its flagship Korea-Green Transformation (K-GX) Strategy, pledging 1,000 trillion won ($747 billion) in public and policy capital through 2035 to structurally decarbonize its carbon-intensive economy. Announced in Seoul at a national conference uniting government ministries and industrial leaders, the package allocates 200 trillion won in direct government spending and over 790 trillion won in specialized climate financing and low-interest credit lines through state institutions. Major industrial giants, including POSCO Holdings, Samsung Electronics, Hanwha Qcells, and LG Electronics, have pledged an additional 220 trillion won in accompanying private-sector transition investments. Central to the plan is an aggressive buildout targeting 100 gigawatts of domestic renewable power generation by 2030, alongside mandating that electric and hydrogen vehicles account for more than 70% of new vehicle sales by 2035.

South Korea's export-driven economy has long grappled with heavy dependence on imported fossil fuels and concentrated industrial emissions from steelmaking, petrochemicals, semiconductors, and refining. Under the K-GX roadmap, state authorities are prioritizing direct industrial transformation, including piloting a 300,000-ton hydrogen-reduced ironmaking commercial demonstration by 2030 and expanding high-voltage direct-current transmission grids. Officials emphasized that the policy serves as a vital safeguard for national industrial competitiveness amid global green trade barriers like carbon border taxes. Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il stated that the K-GX program represents 'an absolute structural rebirth of Korea's industrial landscape,' insisting the state must empower domestic manufacturing champions to act as market architects rather than followers in the global clean-tech arena.

The initiative signals a major inflection point for energy transition across the Asia-Pacific region, where fossil-importing powerhouses are accelerating domestic clean-power deployment to hedge against volatile oil and gas markets. By backing technologies such as tandem solar cells, small modular nuclear reactors, green hydrogen reduction, and advanced battery chemistry, Seoul aims to capture high-value export supply chains while meeting its mid-century net-zero commitments. Over the coming months, parliamentary committees will finalize regulatory relief frameworks and launch the first public-private contract tenders for offshore grid infrastructure and clean hydrogen distribution hubs.
