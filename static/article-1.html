Samsung Posts Record $80B Profit as AI Memory Boom Drives Historic Ninefold Surge

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 08, 2026 at 15:17 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Reuters
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Reuters editorial archive
Executive Briefing: Samsung Electronics has projected a record third-quarter operating profit of 107.4 trillion won ($80.2 billion), marking a nearly ninefold surge driven by relentless global demand for artificial intelligence memory chips. The milestone makes Samsung the first technology company in history to exceed 100 trillion won in quarterly operating profit, reflecting an ongoing structural shortage of high-bandwidth memory components. The results cement hardware and semiconductor manufacturers as the premier financial beneficiaries of the expanding generative AI infrastructure cycle.

SEOUL — South Korean technology powerhouse Samsung Electronics issued blockbuster guidance for its third-quarter earnings on Thursday, projecting consolidated operating profit of approximately 107.4 trillion won ($80.2 billion)—an extraordinary 782.5% leap compared to the same period last year. Third-quarter consolidated sales surged nearly 127% year-on-year to 195 trillion won ($145.6 billion), breaking company records for a fourth consecutive quarter. The preliminary figures cleared market consensus tracked by LSEG SmartEstimate and established Samsung as the world's first technology company to ever breach the 100-trillion-won quarterly operating threshold.

The unprecedented financial windfall has been propelled by severe supply deficits across high-performance memory silicon, as hyperscale data center operators, cloud providers, and AI accelerator builders aggressively compete for available allocation. Robust pricing across conventional DRAM and enterprise NAND storage, combined with rapid adoption and mass shipments of advanced sixth-generation High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4) architectures, accounted for the lion's share of operating earnings. However, the astronomical earnings also highlighted acute divergence within Samsung's own operations: while its core semiconductor business printed record profits, its mobile device and consumer electronics divisions endured mounting margin pressure due to the escalating procurement costs of memory hardware.

The historic print highlights a fundamental macro shift as supply constraints across the global semiconductor ecosystem show few signs of abating. Industry leaders, including Samsung and competitor Micron Technology, have signaled that the acute imbalance between AI-driven compute demand and memory production capacity could persist through 2028. As elevated hardware costs increasingly weigh on consumer device margins and inflate data center buildout capital expenditures worldwide, market participants now await Samsung's full division-by-division audited financial breakdown, scheduled for release on October 29.
