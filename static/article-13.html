Trump Strikes Russian Diesel Pact With Putin, Triggering Outrage in Kyiv and Allied Capitals

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 10, 2026 at 10:50 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Axios
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Axios editorial archive
Executive Briefing: U.S. President Donald Trump has reached an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin to import millions of tons of Russian diesel, granting sanctions waivers to ease soaring domestic fuel prices ahead of the midterm elections. The sudden policy shift has provoked severe backlash from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and NATO allies, who condemned the deal as an unjustified concession that undermines allied pressure on Moscow.

In a dramatic geopolitical reversal, U.S. President Donald Trump announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin to facilitate the delivery of Russian diesel fuel to American and international markets, accompanied by immediate sanctions relief. Under the terms disclosed by the White House, Russia will initially supply over 300,000 metric tons of diesel, scaling up to an additional 500,000 tons in November, 1 million tons thereafter, and potentially up to 3 million tons depending on refinery output. To execute the transaction, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) promptly issued a temporary general license authorizing the sale and delivery of Russian refined products, marking the first major easing of Washington's wartime energy embargo against Moscow.

The decision comes against the backdrop of acute global energy volatility, compounded by shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz and a domestic surge in diesel prices, which reached historic highs above $6.50 per gallon in parts of the United States ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy reacted with scathing criticism, labeling the pact 'not fair and not honest' and warning that providing the Kremlin with fresh petroleum revenues represents an investment in prolonged warfare rather than peace. Ukrainian officials also alleged that exploratory peace discussions held in Florida by U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were used as a diplomatic cover, particularly after Washington repeatedly urged Kyiv to cease drone strikes on Russian refining facilities.

The unilateral arrangement has sent shockwaves through transatlantic relations, triggering sharp pushback from European allies and congressional lawmakers who argue the move fractures the unified sanctions regime established following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. While wholesale diesel futures in New York declined roughly 4.5% immediately following the announcement, market strategists question whether the volume will structurally solve broader supply constraints. As diplomatic tensions escalate, the agreement threatens to upend ongoing negotiations over post-war security architecture and signals an unpredictable realignment in Washington's strategic posture toward both Eastern Europe and global energy markets.
