Hurricane Isaias Slams US Gulf Coast as Category 3 Storm, Cutting Power to 860,000

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 10, 2026 at 10:52 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: The Guardian
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to The Guardian editorial archive
Executive Briefing: Hurricane Isaias roared ashore along the Florida Panhandle and Alabama early Saturday as a dangerous Category 3 storm, leaving over 860,000 customers without electricity and halting 63% of Gulf crude oil production. Fueled by abnormally warm 30°C ocean waters, the tempest marks the latest first Atlantic hurricane in over a century, delivering fresh evidence of climate-driven extreme weather volatility.

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin on the western Florida Panhandle late Friday before churning inland into southern Alabama on Saturday morning, unleashing destructive 115 mph (185 km/h) winds, severe coastal storm surges, and torrential downpours. The National Hurricane Center reported the storm weakened to a post-tropical system as its core tracked south of Montgomery, but emergency authorities warned that severe flash flood and tornado hazards persist across the Southeast. Over 860,000 utility customers across Florida and Alabama remain plunged in darkness as fallen trees and toppled utility pylons litter roadways from Pensacola to Mobile.

Energy infrastructure across the Gulf Coast sustained sweeping operational pauses. Federal regulators and energy operators confirmed that 130 offshore platforms were evacuated, shuttering approximately 1.3 million barrels per day—or 63%—of the Gulf of Mexico's crude oil extraction alongside 1.1 billion cubic feet of natural gas output. Major producers including Chevron enacted protective shutdowns across multiple installations while monitoring refinery operations along the coast. Local officials, including Alabama Emergency Management Agency meteorologists, urged residents to shelter in place as coastal storm surges reached up to six feet, inundating barrier islands and tearing roofs off commercial buildings.

Meteorologists and climate scientists emphasized that Isaias's explosive rapid intensification was supercharged by sea surface temperatures hovering at a scorching 86°F (30°C)—thermal levels made hundreds of times more probable by greenhouse gas emissions. Remarkably, Isaias is the Atlantic basin's first hurricane of the 2026 season, tying the historical benchmark set in 1905 for the latest first hurricane ever recorded. As the storm tracks northward into the Tennessee Valley, the climate anomaly underscores urgent warnings issued by global negotiators at this week's pre-COP31 summit regarding the intensifying unpredictability of extreme weather in an overheated global climate system.
