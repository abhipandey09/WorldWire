Russian Strike on Passenger Buses Kills at Least 30 in Ukraine's Kramatorsk

✍️ By Abhishek Pandey, WorldWire Desk  |  ⏱️ Published October 08, 2026 at 15:42 UTC  |  📰 Wire Attribution: Associated Press
Photojournalism wire visual • Attributed to Associated Press editorial archive
Executive Briefing: A Russian aerial guided bomb struck two passenger buses in the frontline eastern Ukrainian city of Kramatorsk on Thursday, killing at least 30 people and wounding 18 others. The devastating morning attack forced local authorities to suspend public transit across key strongholds in the Donetsk region as Moscow ramps up its pre-winter offensive.

A Russian guided bomb ripped through two passenger buses near a transit stop on Yuvileina Street in the eastern Ukrainian city of Kramatorsk on Thursday morning, killing at least 30 civilians and injuring 18. The regional prosecutor's office opened a war-crimes investigation into the strike, which officials said was likely carried out using a UMPB D-30SN aerial glide bomb deployed during peak commuting hours. Emergency services described scenes of extensive devastation, with charred vehicle chassis and victims scattered across the road. In response to the attack and follow-up drone activity in the area, Donetsk regional governor Vadym Filashkin ordered the immediate suspension of all public transport in Kramatorsk and neighboring Sloviansk to prevent further loss of life.

The deadly strike comes on the heels of another devastating barrage just 24 hours earlier in the northern town of Pryluky, where a residential building strike killed 22 people, including five children. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy condemned the Kramatorsk bombing as a deliberate assault on ordinary civilians, declaring on Telegram that Moscow is attempting to eradicate life across the Donbas and warning that the attack 'will not go unanswered'. Russian defense officials offered no immediate comment on the bus strike, while Ukrainian authorities reiterated calls for the remaining civilian population in front-line districts to evacuate westward.

The atrocity represents one of the single deadliest civilian casualty incidents in Ukraine in recent months and underscores an intensifying Russian campaign targeting civilian logistics, transport links, and municipal energy networks as winter approaches. With nationwide aerial strikes already triggering worsening emergency blackouts in Kyiv and surrounding regions, Ukrainian officials have renewed urgent pleas to Western allies for additional air defense interceptors. International monitors caution that Russia's expanding use of standoff glide bombs against urban centers poses a severe challenge to existing air shield capabilities along the front.
